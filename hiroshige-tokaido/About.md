# About this collection

*Fifty-three Stations of the Tōkaidō* (東海道五十三次) is Utagawa Hiroshige's (歌川広重, 1797–1858) series of colour woodblock prints, published by Hōeidō (保永堂) in 1833–34. It has fifty-five designs: Nihonbashi, the fifty-three post stations of the Tōkaidō, and Kyoto. Museums often catalogue it as the "Hoeido Tokaido" to tell it from Hiroshige's later Tōkaidō series.

The images come from the Art Institute of Chicago, which releases them into the public domain under [CC0 1.0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/), so they are free to use. The prints are also old enough to be in the public domain whatever the museum says: the artist died in 1858. Each print page links to its record at the museum.

Distances are in ri, the Edo-period unit, from the running sum of station-to-station distances in the Japanese Wikipedia route table, which cites the Edo-period register 宿村大概帳. One ri is about 3.9 km. Station positions come from the same table and were checked against many of the individual station articles, but they are approximate, and the coastline on the map is hand-drawn.

The one-sentence notes and the "what to look for" lines were written for this site from the titles and what the prints show. The museum's record has no description of its own.
